16Plan: Groupwise Maximality as an Approachability Problem

Presenter: So, once again, we want to frame this as an approachability problem. We wish to construct the halfspace response, and we'll use the antisymmetry of the pairwise margin matrix to simplify this. And once we show that we have a halfspace response, we can apply Blackwell's approachability guarantee and finish off from there. And this avoids having to actually make any minimax computation. You'll see what that looks like.

So the coordinates of our really long vector, our \(g\), are going to be group and fixed-challenger pairs: the group and then the completion or alternative. So big \(K\) there, not to be confused with the convex set, is the number of groups that we have, and \(m\) is the number of alternatives, the prompt completions we're competing against. And so you can imagine that, as Blackwell does what it does, for every group we will have low error against every fixed challenger. If all the entries are low, then you know that for every group, the policy can't lose to any fixed \(b\).

And what is our target set? In this case, it'll be the negative orthant. In other words, it's kind of like the high-dimensional third quadrant, where everything is negative. Why? Well, if we lie within this negative orthant, we know that we are not being pairwise beaten by any fixed comparison: every violation is at most zero. And what's neat is that this projection has a closed form, because you can just take all of the positive components and zero them out, which we'll get to in a second.

And our response is a policy whose weighted violation is at most zero for whatever antisymmetric comparison matrix we plug into it. And this \(M\) is our pairwise response matrix. Antisymmetry will once again make this cleaner in a second.

And what is our data? We will estimate this \(M\) matrix, this pairwise comparison matrix, from just one observed comparison per round. So we'll sample a user, see a comparison, and then we'll create an estimate of what this true \(M\) would be, in a way that preserves the antisymmetry of the matrix. And then our guarantees will follow from there: from groupwise maximality on the sampled sequence to guarantees on the distribution. That's just the plan.
